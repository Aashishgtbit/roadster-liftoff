October 15, 2026 SpaceX test site, McGregor, Texas

The Roadster left the ground.

Nine years after it was first shown, Tesla's second Roadster climbed a ramp on suction, hung upside down, and hopped a row of cars on cold-gas thrusters.

The car was operated remotely; no one sat inside for the stunts. Fans under the floor pulled it onto a curved ramp and held it there upside down. It let go, flipped, and set itself down on air jets. For the finale it launched over parked cars and a semi-truck with a roll in the middle, and landed clean on the far side.

The demo was real. Most of the numbers around it come from Tesla and Elon Musk, and none of them have been tested independently yet.

What the car did

  1. Up the wall on suction

    Fans underneath pulled the car onto a steep curved ramp and kept it there past vertical.

  2. Upside down at the top

    The suction held it inverted, about 12 m up, before it crept on to the end.

  3. Off the end, flip, land

    It tipped off, rolled half a turn, and lowered itself to the ground on its jets.

  4. Over the lineup

    The finale: a hop over the parked cars and a semi, a roll in mid-air, and a soft landing.

Nine years to liftoff

  1. 2008

    The original Roadster goes on sale. It is Tesla's first car.

  2. Nov 2017

    The second Roadster is revealed as a surprise at the Tesla Semi event, with a claimed 1.9 s 0–60.

  3. Feb 2018

    Musk's own first-generation Roadster rides a Falcon Heavy into orbit around the Sun.

  4. Jun 2018

    Musk teases a "SpaceX package" with about ten small cold-gas thrusters.

  5. Sep 28, 2026

    The October 1 unveiling is called off for weather; Tesla says the show can only be held outdoors.

  6. Oct 15, 2026

    The unveiling at a SpaceX test site in Texas. The car leaves the ground.

  7. 2027

    Musk has pointed to production starting in 2027. Tesla has not confirmed a date or a price.

How the simulation works

Everything on the other page is computed, not animated by hand. Masses and thrusts are our estimates.

A 1,900 kg body on four springs

A rigid body with its own inertia for roll, pitch and yaw. Each wheel has a spring, a damper and a tyre that grips up to 1.15 g.

Ten nozzles, each pushing from its own spot

Four under the floor (up to 18 kN each), four in the diffuser, two in the nose. Firing one side harder turns the car. Valves take about 40 ms to respond.

A flight computer for the demos

It lifts off, rolls, cancels sideways drift, holds the heading with the nose jets and follows a glide path down to about 2 m/s.

Suction for the ramp

Fans pull the floor toward the wall with up to 30 kN, 1.6 times the car's weight, which is enough to drive past vertical and stop upside down.

Sound made from the numbers

Nothing is recorded. Motor whine follows wheel speed, the hiss follows total thrust, valves pop as they open and landings thump.

Track Mode wing

Road, High-Downforce and Low-Drag, as in Tesla's video. Each mode changes the downforce in the physics.
