The demo

The night, beat by beat

There was no driver in the car for the stunts. Reports say it was operated remotely.

  1. 1

    Up the ramp on suction

    Fans underneath pulled the car onto a steep ramp, the same downforce trick racing cars have flirted with for decades.

  2. 2

    Upside down

    The suction held the car inverted on the ramp before it let go.

  3. 3

    Spin, flip, land

    It spun off the ramp, flipped, and used air jets to set itself down.

  4. 4

    Over the lineup

    The finale: a launch over parked cars and a semi-truck, with a mid-air flip, and a clean landing on the far side.

Under the hood of this page

How the simulation works

The jump above is computed, not keyframed. Masses and thrusts are our estimates, not Tesla figures.

  1. 1

    A 1,900 kg body on four springs

    The car is a rigid body with real inertia for roll, pitch and yaw. Each wheel has its own spring and damper and a tyre that grips up to 1.15 g, so you can see the suspension unload during the spool and compress on landing.

  2. 2

    Ten nozzles, each with its own force

    Four floor nozzles near the wheel arches (up to 18 kN each), four in the diffuser and two in the nose. Every nozzle pushes at its own position, so firing one side harder spins the car. Valves take about 40 ms to respond.

  3. 3

    A flight computer flies the stunt

    The car on the night was remote-operated. Here a controller does the job. On the jump it lifts off, rolls 360° along the direction of travel, uses even bursts while the car is on its side to cancel sideways drift, holds the heading with the nose jets, and follows a glide path down to touch the ground at about 2 m/s.

  4. 4

    The ramp: suction does the climbing

    Fans under the floor pull the car onto a curved wall with up to 30 kN, 1.6 times its weight. That is enough grip to drive up past vertical and pause upside down at about 12 m. Then, as in Tesla's video, it creeps on with the diffuser jets helping and tips off the end of the runway upside down. Past the runway it rolls 180° about its own length, an x-axis flip, so it comes up on its wheels facing the opposite way to the runway. It hovers slowly down on its floor jets, with the diffuser and nose jets holding position, settles at the foot of the ramp at under 1 m/s and drives away.

  5. 5

    Sound from the numbers

    Nothing is recorded. The motor whine follows wheel speed, the hiss and roar follow total thrust, each valve pops as it opens, the fans whine with suction, and landings thump harder the faster the car comes down. Volume and stereo pan follow the camera.

  6. 6

    Track Mode aero

    The wing button cycles the three modes from Tesla's video. Road stows the wing, High-Downforce raises and pitches it, Low-Drag raises it flat. Each mode changes the downforce in the simulation.

The lineup

Three trims, two of them add-ons

Tesla treats Thrust and Rocket as augments stacked on the Performance car.

Base car

Performance

The two-seat sports car itself. Everything else bolts on top of this.

Augment

Thrust

Adds the cold-gas thruster system for extra launch and handling.

Not road legal

Rocket

The full package. Tesla's own page reportedly says it is not approved for public roads.

The long road

From concept to liftoff

  1. 2008

    The original Roadster goes on sale. It is Tesla's first car.

  2. Nov 2017

    The second-gen Roadster is revealed as a surprise at the Tesla Semi event, with a claimed 1.9 s 0–60.

  3. Feb 2018

    Musk's own first-gen Roadster rides a Falcon Heavy into orbit around the Sun.

  4. Jun 2018

    Musk teases a "SpaceX package" with about ten small cold-gas thrusters.

  5. Sep 28, 2026

    The Oct 1 unveiling is called off for weather. Tesla says the show can only be held outdoors.

  6. Oct 15, 2026

    The unveiling happens at a SpaceX test site in Texas, and the car leaves the ground.

  7. 2027?

    Musk has pointed to production starting in 2027. Tesla has not confirmed a date or a price.

Reality check

Seen vs. still a promise

The demo was real. Most of the numbers come from Tesla and Musk.

Seen on the night

  • A car climbing a ramp on suction and hanging upside down
  • A thruster-assisted hop over cars and a semi, with a flip
  • Three trims named: Performance, Thrust, Rocket

Not yet shown

  • Sustained flight. This was a hop, not a flight
  • A human driving it through the stunts
  • Independent tests of the 0.59 s figure
  • A price, a production date, or a street-legal Rocket
